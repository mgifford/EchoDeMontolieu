## Implementation of a video protection in rental

The Mayor, recalls that the council voted on 18 September 2024 the principle of implementing video protection.

However, the quote for this operation in purchase therefore in investment was €37,840.80 including VAT for the entirety of the installation.

The company LEASE PROTECT received last June made a proposal in rental, therefore in operation including 5/6 protection points. They manage the entire file alone including consultations and the management of the various phases of authorisation including those of the architect of the buildings of France.

The proposal includes of course the installation by the company of the cameras, total and unlimited maintenance, and from the 39th month, a technological evolution of the equipment without extra cost. 5 points retained + 1 optional for the swimming pool - Rue de la mairie - Place Paul Lacombe (said “school in the document) - Place de l’église, - Office de Tourisme - Parking de Fraisse

The rates in the document have been reduced, subject to a decision taken before 30 September 2025: the monthly rent of the overall project would go from €859 excluding VAT to €749 (€598 for the project without the swimming pool).

The council votes by 9 votes for and 4 against, the proposal for the rental of the LEASE PROTECT company based on the 5 points, for an amount of 598 € HT per month.

## Pedestrian route to the cooperative museum ceres franco

The councillors received the study carried out by the ATD11. A first meeting took place on the 18th on site in Cérès Franco with the region, the department and Carcassonne Agglo in attendance. Present were, Bernard LAURET, Laure ESCARÉ. The options of the GIP were debated there.

After numerous discussions on the ATD11 document, and a visit to the parking area on the Fraisse road, the partners want to favour a route from said parking area and not that of the Villelongue road: less impactful work, faster and cheaper. A new study must be submitted before the end of the month with an evaluation by the department, in order to allow the municipality to submit subsidy applications before the end of October.

Jeanne ETORÉ-LORTHOLARY considers this choice to be extremely regrettable. She recalls that since the start of the work at the Cooperative, Jean-Luc Olivier and herself have drawn the council's attention to the need to provide for the development of a car park for the museum and a pathway, not only from this car park to the museum, but from this car park, to the museum and to the centre of the village.

It seems to her that an establishment on which 5 million of work has just been invested deserves a dedicated car park and pathway and not a cut-price solution, that it deserves a link and a direct pathway to the centre of the village rather than with the city stadium.

She has repeatedly requested the council to purchase the land on the other side of the road, and there is still one remaining.

Bernard LAURET replies that the municipality will not purchase building land to make a car park, and that the land in question, located upstream of the Alzonne road bridge, would in any case have required significant work to secure passage over the latter. Jeanne ETORÉ-LORTHOLARY recalls that in the study transmitted by the ATD11, there was the possibility of a pathway on the right-hand side of the departmental road.

Laure ESCARÉ points out that this pathway generated significant technical difficulties: burying of lines, moving of poles and walls, to be rebuilt further away, the cost of which was not negligible.

The other two options required land purchases, subject to agreement with the owners.

Jeanne ETORÉ-LORTHOLARY considers that at a minimum they should have been consulted. It is reminded to him that one of these owners who had been contacted for the development of the container collection area, had not responded to our request for an emphyteutic lease. Éric OLIVIER estimates the proposed operation originally at, at least, 400,000 euros. For the footbridge alone, allowing the bridge to be doubled with PMR access, at the price per m², it comes to 80,000€. Aurélie BARBERO and Céline SALA regret that the Villelongue car park cannot be developed. Laure ESCARÉ replies that a minimum development of the car park and signage work to slow down arrival on the bridge can very well be considered later, this does not prevent work on the pedestrian route chosen by the partners.

The intersections must be developed with "meeting platforms", useful for slowing down vehicles, especially those coming down from the village towards Alzonne. The bridge known as "des sœurs" will also be secured more easily. It is much wider than the one on the Alzonne road, and can easily be upgraded to meet PMR standards for pedestrians. The presence of toilets in the car park, a city stadium, and after landscaping by the municipality of a picnic area, are assets highlighted by the partners present at the meeting

on the ground. The work being less heavy, we can reasonably hope that it can be completed when the museum opens. [p.5](https://www.montolieu.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/CRCM-25092025-site.pdf#page=5)

Jeanne ETORÉ-LORTHOLARY considers that the route as envisaged between the Fraisse road and the Alzonne road is completely off-center and in a way cuts the project off from the rest of the actors of the Village du Livre et des Arts.

Laure ESCARÉ points out that the Villelongue road car park is even more off-center from this point of view.

The Fraisse road car park is also already used by many tourists who walk to the heart of the village.

The council votes by 10 votes in favour and three against, the project of the route chosen by the partners.

## Opinion on the draft PPRI

The document was sent to the councillors, and was made available at the town hall.

As part of the revision of the Flood Risk Prevention Plan (PPRI) of the Fresquel watershed, the municipality was requested by the Prefecture services to provide an opinion within a period of two months.

As a reminder, once approved, the PPRI applies to planning documents and has direct effects on building rights. It defines risk zones in which the possibilities of developing, building or renovating are restricted, or even prohibited, depending on the level of exposure to risk.

The council gives a favourable opinion on the PPRI project unanimously.
